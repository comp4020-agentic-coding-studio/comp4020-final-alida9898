# Crit 10 reflection

**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

The turning point this week was deciding to add accounts. My own rules
said "no login, the guest link is enough", but the guest link could not
really tell two people apart, and if someone got the owner's link, the
town was basically theirs. I thought: isn't that a bug? So I decided to
make accounts. It is simpler and more like common sense.

At first I worried it went against why I made this game, but it doesn't.
I just want a zen game — having an account doesn't make it less zen. Then
there's no need for invite links: you just add a friend, and you can go
into their town, like in every game people already play. I wanted
becoming friends to need both people to agree, and I was unsure whether a
non-friend should be able to visit, so I had the agent research how
Animal Crossing, Stardew Valley and Minecraft do it. They all separate
"can look" from "can change", so anyone can visit a town but only a
friend can help place things.

**What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?**

I think looking up precedents is the right thing to do, because there are
still many examples to learn from, including the mistakes people made
before us. Even now that we have AI, there is still a huge amount of
other people's experience out there, and because we are human, our way of
thinking is probably closer to theirs. We should avoid the pits that
people before us already fell into, so looking up precedents still
matters a lot.

I also want to keep my own judgement. I can't trust AI completely.
